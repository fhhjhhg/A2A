## 2024-05-24 - [Parallelizing I/O-bound operations in Jupyter Notebooks]
**Learning:** Parallelizing I/O-bound shell commands (e.g., `wget`, `tar`) in Jupyter Notebooks should use `ThreadPoolExecutor` with `subprocess.run` instead of IPython `!` magic, which is not thread-safe and executes sequentially in loops. When using `subprocess.Popen` to chain commands via pipes (e.g., piping `wget` to `tar`), ensure you explicitly call `.wait()` on the upstream process after calling `.communicate()` on the downstream process, before evaluating the upstream process's `.returncode`. This prevents race conditions where the upstream process has not fully terminated. Additionally, when measuring performance is impractical in a sandbox (e.g., due to massive 14GB+ downloads or network timeouts), provide a theoretical rationale explaining why the architectural change (like parallelizing blocking I/O) guarantees a net performance improvement.
**Action:** Use `ThreadPoolExecutor` to parallelize independent I/O tasks like downloading multiple files, and use `subprocess.Popen` carefully with proper `.communicate()` and `.wait()` sequences to pipe commands.
